About this report

About this report
This document is a sample. It demonstrates the format, method and standard of a
report of proceedings prepared from the recordings of a scientific meeting. The
meeting it describes, the Hartwell Cardiovascular Innovation Forum 2026, is
fictional, as are the society, the people, the companies, the products, the trials,
the figures and the sources referred to throughout. No statement in this report
concerns any real organisation, product or person.
Purpose and scope
A report of this kind sets out what was presented in each session of a meeting, draws together
the subjects that recurred across sessions, and distinguishes throughout between what
speakers demonstrated with data and what they asserted. The sample covers ten sessions of a
half-day meeting: opening and closing remarks, six sessions of company presentations with
panel discussion, a keynote lecture by a regulator and an investor panel. Posters and
networking sessions are not covered.

How to read this report
The executive summary sets out twelve principal findings. Part One presents eight thematic
analyses, each of which follows a single subject across the whole meeting. Part Two is a record
of each session in programme order. An assessment of the evidence presented, a calendar of
the dates that speakers gave for forthcoming results, and a chapter on developments
announced after the meeting follow Part One.

Status of the statements in this report
Statements attributed to a company are that company's own account of its products and
results, as given by its representative on the day. Their inclusion does not constitute
endorsement by the society. Those that could be checked against a public source, such as a
trial registry, a regulator's website or a company's own announcement, were checked: where
the public source differs from what was said, the text carries a footnote naming the source.
Statements for which no public source exists, including unpublished data, forecasts and
opinions, rest on the speaker alone. Statements attributed to an individual are that individual's
view. The keynote speaker stated that she was giving her personal views.
Results from animal or laboratory studies are described as preclinical wherever they appear.
Where a figure was shown on a slide and was not spoken, the text says so.

Footnotes
Footnotes are used for one purpose: to record a discrepancy. Where a speaker's words differed
from the speaker's own slide, where two figures were given for the same thing, where one
speaker contradicted another, where a statement was clarified after review of the recording, or
where a public source differs from what was said, the main text gives the version treated as
primary, which is normally what was said, and the footnote gives the other version.

Executive summary
The Hartwell Cardiovascular Innovation Forum 2026 brought companies,
investors, clinicians and a regulator into one room for an afternoon, on the
premise that those who fund, develop, regulate and use cardiovascular
treatments rarely meet openly. The product closest to routine use was a device
rather than a drug, and the clinicians on every panel returned to the same
qualification: longer-acting treatment changes the burden of care without
removing it.
Opening the meeting, Professor Annelise Dahlberg identified durability of lipid-lowering
treatment as a challenge that "remains still a big challenge" and described inherited
cardiomyopathy as an area "turning into real treatment options". The presentations that
followed bore both statements out. Orrinvale Medical announced a CE mark for a leadless
dual-chamber pacemaker; Halden Pharmaceuticals reported that its oral factor XI inhibitor
had met its primary endpoint in a phase 3 trial of 11,400 patients; Vantrel Bio reported
lipoprotein(a) lowering above 90% with an outcomes trial fully enrolled. Elsewhere the
evidence was earlier: gene therapy for inherited cardiomyopathy rested on cohorts of 7 to
12 patients, and four of the eight emerging companies had no human data at all. The only
regulator on the programme, Ilse Vandermeer, spoke in a personal capacity; the positions
of regulators and payers were otherwise reported by companies and clinicians.

Lipid-lowering and atherosclerosis

Lipid-lowering and atherosclerosis
Five companies presented in the lipid session, and between them they described
three answers to the problem Professor Annelise Dahlberg had named in her
opening remarks: a twice-yearly injection against lipoprotein(a), a once-daily oral
inhibitor of PCSK9, and a once-yearly injection against the same target. The
figures were large and the comparators were placebo. The clinicians on the panel
accepted the biology and questioned the follow-up.
Overview
Rachel Adeyemi of Vantrel Bio set out the case for lipoprotein(a) as a target: a survey of
cardiologists found that 63% never test for it, and the company's phase 2 study reported a
reduction of 94% at week 48. 1 Jonas Keller of Marlowe Pharma reported a 61% reduction
in LDL cholesterol at week 12 with the oral inhibitor pendrelvarin, and Miriam Castellanos of
Arden Therapeutics reported that a single dose of ancelsiran held LDL cholesterol 52%
lower at 12 months. , 2 Tobias Hartmann of Solenne Biologics presented an antibody against
ANGPTL3, and Priya Raman of Ravensworth Medical a coronary sinus reducer for refractory
angina. No outcomes data were presented by any company; the one outcomes trial
described, SILVERBIRCH-1, reads out in the second half of 2027.

Lipoprotein(a) is the new target, and the outcomes are two years away
Vantrel's position, raised in several sessions, is that a large population is untreated because
it is untested. Adeyemi reported that 88% of patients in the phase 2 BIRCHWOOD study of
240 patients reached a lipoprotein(a) level below 125 nmol/L, and that the phase 3
SILVERBIRCH-1 trial of 8,240 patients, with a primary endpoint of major adverse
cardiovascular events, is fully enrolled with a readout "late next year". 3 A substudy in 120
patients reported a 9% fall in plaque volume. , 4
On the panel Professor Oskar Weiland asked whether plaque regression translates into
fewer events. Adeyemi said that it is a hypothesis. That exchange is the state of the
evidence: a biomarker lowered by more than 90%, a structural change of single figures, and
no outcome yet. Kenji Okada of Harbourline Capital, speaking on the investor panel, named
lipids as a crowded indication in which understanding one's place in the landscape is the
critical question.

Oral and annual dosing compete on the same target
Two companies presented PCSK9 inhibition in forms that do not resemble each other.
Marlowe's pendrelvarin is a once-daily tablet. The phase 2b LANTANA study of 410 patients
reported the 61% reduction above, and three phase 3 studies, GRANITE-1, GRANITE-2 and
GRANITE-3, began in the third quarter of 2026 on top of statin therapy, with readouts in
2028. Keller described them as "about 2,400 patients each". 5
Arden's ancelsiran is a once-yearly injection. The phase 2 WINTERGREEN study of 112
patients reported the 52% reduction at 12 months after a single dose, and the phase 3
FALCONER study of 3,000 patients is to start in the first quarter of 2027, shown on the
slide. Castellanos stated that patients from the phase 1 study have 7 years of follow-up. 6
Two company videos were played. In the first, four named clinicians stated that 95% of
patients achieve annual dosing and 93% prefer it to monthly injections, without naming a
trial; the second carried patient quotations with the on-screen notice that the voices were
actor portrayals. , 7 On the panel Castellanos said that patients with 6 years of follow-up are
on their sixth annual dose and that the company has "seen up to 20 doses" in the openlabel cohort, which the follow-up period does not allow. , 8

Key point. The headline reductions in this chapter cannot be ranked against one
another. Each was measured at a different time point against placebo in a
different population, and only one programme has an outcomes trial under way.

Monitoring remains, whatever the interval
The point raised most often by clinicians, in this session and in the arrhythmia session, was
that a longer interval between doses is not an interval between visits. Professor Sofia
Esteves stated that no data support leaving a patient unseen for six months, and that in her
reading of the earlier data about half of patients in phase 2 needed uptitration. 9 Professor
Elena Marchetti put the proportion of patients who do not reach their LDL goal on a statin
alone at "at least 30 to 40%", without a source. Nobody proposed abandoning statins, and
the consensus of the panel was that combinations and longer dosing intervals are additions
to the foundation rather than replacements for it.
Vantrel's own real-world claim cut both ways. Adeyemi reported that 47% of patients on
injectable lipid therapy are non-persistent by year five, citing a 2025 analysis. 10 The figure
supports the case for a less frequent injection, and it also describes the population a less
frequent injection will be given to.

Developments since the meeting

Developments since the meeting
This chapter records announcements made after the meeting on 14 October 2026
that bear directly on statements in this report. Each is taken from a public source,
which is named, and none formed part of what was presented in Lisbon.
Results published after the meeting
Tabrexaban in atrial fibrillation. At the meeting Halden Pharmaceuticals reported that
OSPREY-AF had met its primary endpoint and that major bleeding was "nearly halved", with
full results embargoed. According to a company release dated 24 October 2026, the rate of
stroke or systemic embolism was 1.21% per year with tabrexaban against 1.16% with the
comparator (hazard ratio 1.04, within the non-inferiority margin), major bleeding was 1.9%
against 3.1% per year, and discontinuation for adverse events was 4.2% against 4.0%.
These figures were read in a syndicated copy of the release and have not been confirmed
against the company's own publication; they should be confirmed with Halden before
publication.
EBW-100 in Danon disease. On 20 October 2026 Ebbwater Genetics published 24-month
data showing stabilisation of left ventricular wall thickness in 5 of 7 treated patients. The
slide shown at the meeting stated 6 of 6 evaluable patients at 12 months.

Regulatory position at the date of this report
Tovacamten. On 28 October 2026 Thornbury Pharma stated that the US regulator had
accepted its filing for tovacamten with priority review, with a target action date of 12 March
2027. The product is not approved in any jurisdiction.
Harrowgate Therapeutics. The company has stated that it plans a rolling submission later

Method and verification

Method and verification
This appendix describes how a report of this kind is prepared from the recordings
of a meeting and the checks that are applied at each stage. The figures given are
those of the sample.
Sources
The source material is the video recording of each session, amounting in this sample to
approximately four and a half hours. The official programme published by the society is used to
confirm the names and affiliations of moderators, speakers and panel members. Public sources
consulted to verify statements are identified in the footnotes in which they are used.

Transcription and review
The audio of each recording is transcribed by speech recognition software. Each transcript is
then compared with the video. Speakers are identified from the captions shown on screen, and
the text of every slide is read from the recording so that names, product and trial designations,
and figures can be checked against what was displayed. Passages that remain uncertain are
extracted as short clips and reviewed by a member of the project team, who listens to each and
determines the reading. The outcome of every such review is recorded. In this sample, 67 clips
were reviewed.

Extraction of facts
Each transcript is divided into its individual talks and discussions, forty in this sample. Each is
read separately, in isolation from the others, so that statements made by one speaker cannot
be attributed to another. From each, the individual facts are recorded: the products and
mechanisms described, the trials and results reported, the dates given, the regulatory and
commercial statements made, and the opinions and challenges voiced. Each fact is recorded
with the name of the speaker, the time in the recording and the exact words on which it rests. In
this sample this produced a base of 1,641 facts.
Two automated checks are applied. The first confirms that the words quoted in support of each
fact appear verbatim in the transcript. The second confirms that every paragraph of every
transcript has either given rise to at least one fact or been recorded as containing nothing
reportable, so that no part of the meeting is omitted without a decision.

Analysis and drafting
The session records and the thematic analyses are written from the base of facts and not from
the transcripts. Every statement in the working papers carries a reference to the facts on which
it rests. An automated check confirms, for every line, that the facts cited exist, that every figure
in the line appears in those facts in the same form, that anything still unresolved is described as
such, and that anything read from a slide and not spoken is identified as coming from a slide. A
subject is described as having been raised in several sessions only where three or more
separate talks support it.

The chapters of the report are then written from those working papers under the same check.
The references to individual facts are retained in the working files and are omitted from the
printed text for readability.

Verification against public sources
After drafting, every statement that can be tested against a public primary source is tested. The
sources used are, in order of preference, the regulator concerned, the trial registry, the
company's own announcement or regulatory filing, and the peer-reviewed publication. Each
check is limited to sources retrieved on a stated date, and none relies on recollection.
In this sample, 231 checks were made, covering 361 individual statements. Of these, 108 were
confirmed, 74 were confirmed in part, 11 differed from the public source and 38 could not be
found in any public source. A further 149 statements were opinions, forecasts or unpublished
data, for which no public source can exist. Every difference, and every partial confirmation of
importance, is recorded in a footnote that names the source and its date.
The studies named by speakers are matched to their entries on a trial registry and listed in the
following appendix. A study is matched only where the sponsor or product on the registry
corresponds to the company that named it.
Announcements made after the meeting are not blended into the account of what was
presented. They are set out separately in the chapter on developments since the meeting.

Limitations
The report records what was said and shown at the meeting. Verification against public sources
establishes whether a statement agrees with what has been published; it does not establish
that the published information is itself correct, and it cannot reach data that a company has not
made public.
A number of spoken figures do not appear on any slide and could not be checked against a
second source. They are reported as the speaker's statement. In panel discussions the
recording does not always show who is speaking; speakers are identified from the picture and
from the manner in which they are addressed, and a small number of brief interjections cannot
be attributed.
Passages that remain unintelligible after review are omitted. The automated checks confirm
that the report is faithful to the base of facts. They do not determine which findings are the
most important, which is a matter of editorial judgement.
